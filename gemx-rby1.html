gem-x rby1

A fork of NVIDIA's GEM-X, which recovers whole-body 3D human pose from a single camera and retargets it onto a humanoid robot. The upstream pipeline targets a Unitree G1; I'm working on the layer that lets it drive other platforms — which means separating "what the human did" from "what this particular robot can do about it."

the problem

Teaching a humanoid a new motion is expensive. Mocap suits, marker setups, and hand-authored trajectories all scale badly. Monocular pose estimation offers a much cheaper path: point a single ordinary camera at a person, recover their full-body pose, and hand that motion to a robot.

The catch is the handoff. A 77-joint human body model and a humanoid robot do not share a skeleton, joint limits, mass distribution, or degrees of freedom. Retargeting is a constrained mapping problem, not a copy. Upstream solves it for one specific platform; the assumptions of that platform are baked into the mapping. My work is on pulling those assumptions out into something swappable.

how it works

One boundary carries the whole design: everything left of the retargeter is about the human, everything right of it is about a specific robot.

decisions worth defending

01

Extend a strong model, don't retrain one

Reproducing a state-of-the-art pose estimator would have consumed the entire project and produced a worse model. The scarce thing here is the retargeting layer, so that's where the effort goes. Reading someone else's research code well is its own skill.

trade-off: I inherit upstream's failure modes and can't fix pose errors at the source. Acceptable — those are documented, measurable, and not my research question.

02

Keep the seam at the retargeter

Every platform-specific assumption — joint count, limits, link lengths, what's even actuated — lives on one side of a single interface. Adding a robot should mean writing a new mapping, not editing the perception stack.

trade-off: a clean boundary can't exploit platform-specific shortcuts in the estimator. Worth it for a pipeline meant to serve more than one robot.

03

Monocular input as a deliberate constraint

A single RGB camera is strictly harder than mocap or depth — scale and depth are ambiguous from one view. It's also the only setup that works with a phone and a room, which is what makes the approach useful outside a lab.

trade-off: less accurate absolute positioning than multi-camera capture. The retargeter has to be robust to that, not assume it away.

04

Stay mergeable with upstream

Forks of active research repos rot fast. I keep additions in new modules rather than rewriting existing ones, so pulling in upstream improvements stays a routine merge instead of an archaeology project.

trade-off: some duplication and indirection I'd refactor away in a codebase I fully owned.

where this goes

A retargeting quality metric. Right now "does this look right" is a human judgement. End-effector trajectory error against the source pose would make platform comparisons quantitative.

Feasibility filtering. Some human motions simply aren't reachable for a given robot. The retargeter should say so explicitly rather than emitting a clipped approximation.

Closing the loop with simulation. Retargeted motion that's kinematically valid can still be dynamically unstable. Running output through a physics sim before hardware is the obvious next gate.
